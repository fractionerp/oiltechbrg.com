Overview

When a thrust bearing arrives badly damaged and a dependable replacement is needed.

The failed thrust bearing arrived with extensive damage to the working faces of the pads. The whitemetal surfaces showed severe wiping and scoring, with several pads no longer suitable for repair or return to service.

For critical rotating equipment, this type of damage has to be treated as both a repair problem and an engineering problem. The bearing must be inspected carefully, the reusable elements identified, and the replacement parts manufactured so the final assembly matches the required geometry and load-sharing behaviour.

Oiltech Bearings rebuilt the assembly by assessing the failed components, recovering the usable design information, manufacturing a new thrust pad set, and assembling a replacement bearing suitable for installation.

Project Phases

From damaged pads to a complete replacement assembly.

Phase 1 - Inspection & Failure Assessment

Understanding the condition of the failed bearing.

Oiltech inspected the thrust bearing as received, documenting the condition of the pads, carrier, pivots, retaining features and other reusable components. The damaged whitemetal faces were assessed to determine what could be repaired, what needed to be replaced, and what information could be recovered for manufacture.

Phase 2 - Reverse Engineering

Recovering the geometry needed to rebuild the assembly.

Where production drawings are unavailable or incomplete, the existing parts become the engineering reference. The pad geometry, carrier interfaces, support arrangement and assembly clearances were used to define the replacement pad set and ensure compatibility with the original thrust bearing assembly.

Phase 3 - New Pad Manufacture

Manufacturing replacement pads for a damaged thrust bearing.

A complete new pad set was manufactured to suit the bearing carrier. The replacement pads were finished to the required geometry and surface condition, with attention to the working faces, pad support details and fit within the existing assembly.

Phase 4 - Assembly & Final Checks

Bringing the bearing back to an installable condition.

The new thrust pads were assembled into the carrier and checked against the original bearing arrangement. The completed unit was supplied as a clean, practical replacement for the failed assembly, with the damaged bearing retained as a clear reference for the scale of the rebuild.

Repair Insight

A failed bearing can still provide the information needed to rebuild it.

Even when the whitemetal faces are badly damaged, the carrier, pad arrangement and surviving geometry can provide enough information to manufacture a replacement set. The key is knowing which dimensions are evidence, which surfaces are damaged, and where bearing design judgement is needed to complete the rebuild.
