Bearing Terminology

Clear definitions for the materials, bearing forms and lubrication principles used in hydrodynamic bearing work.

Related Terms Are Not Always Synonyms

A bearing can be described by its running material, supported load, mechanical form and lubrication principle at the same time.

Running material

Whitemetal / Babbitt

What the lined running surface is made from.

Load direction

Journal or Thrust

Whether the bearing supports radial or axial load.

Bearing form

Sleeve or Tilting Pad

How the load-carrying surface is constructed.

Operating principle

Hydrodynamic Fluid Film

How lubricant pressure separates the moving surfaces.

Technical Glossary

Find a Bearing Term

36 terms
Material

Aluminium-Tin / Aluminum-Tin

An aluminium-based bearing alloy containing tin, normally used as a lining on a stronger backing. It can provide higher fatigue and temperature capability than conventional Babbitt in suitable designs. See the bearing material selection guide.

Material

Babbitt / Babbit

Babbitt, with two final t's, is the established name for a family of soft bearing alloys associated with Isaac Babbitt's original patent. Babbit is a common search spelling. In industrial bearing use, Babbitt is broadly equivalent to whitemetal, but the precise tin- or lead-based grade still matters.

Construction

Backing / Bearing Back

The stronger component supporting the bearing lining. It may be steel, bronze, copper chrome zirconium or another engineered material. The backing provides structure and can strongly affect heat transfer and distortion.

Material

Bearing Metal / Lining

The material forming the running surface of a lined bearing. In Oiltech's core applications this is normally a bonded layer of whitemetal or Babbitt on a stronger backing. See the whitemetal lining thickness guide for design and repair considerations.

Monitoring

Bearing Metal Temperature

Temperature measured by a sensor embedded in or beneath the bearing lining. It differs from supply oil, drain oil and local surface temperature, and depends strongly on sensor position and depth. See the bearing temperature-limits guide.

Construction

Bimetal Bearing

A bearing construction combining two principal metallic layers, commonly a structural steel backing with a bonded bearing-alloy lining. The exact lining, bond and layer thickness must still be specified.

Material

Bronze Bearing

A plain-bearing surface made from a copper-based alloy, sometimes as a solid component and sometimes in a multilayer construction. Bronze is generally stronger but less conformable and less able to embed debris than Babbitt.

Geometry

Clearance

The designed difference between bearing bore and shaft size. Radial clearance is the gap on one side with the shaft centred; diametral clearance is twice the radial value. State which one is being used.

Material requirement

Copper-Free Whitemetal

A procurement or engineering description for a soft bearing alloy with restricted copper content, not one universal grade. The maximum copper limit, complete chemistry, in-scope components and verification method must be stated. See the copper-free whitemetal guide.

Material

Copper-Lead

A copper-based bearing alloy containing a lead phase. It may be used as a running layer or beneath a softer overlay. It is not automatically a tri-metal bearing, and oil chemistry and corrosion exposure require review.

Thrust bearing

Collar / Runner

The rotating surface that runs against the thrust bearing pads. Collar is common when the thrust surface is integral with or fitted to the shaft; runner is also used for the rotating mating component.

Lubrication

Directed Lubrication

A controlled oil-supply arrangement that delivers fresh lubricant to the leading edge or inlet of individual pads, reducing hot-oil carryover, churning loss and temperature compared with some flooded arrangements.

Geometry

End Float / End Play

The permitted axial movement of a shaft between its thrust-control surfaces. It is an assembly and machine-system value, not simply a pad or lining dimension.

Thrust bearing

Equalising Thrust Bearing

A tilting-pad thrust bearing with a mechanical support system that helps distribute load between pads and accommodate limited misalignment. Non-equalising bearings rely more directly on accurate pad and collar geometry.

Bearing form

Fixed-Profile Bearing

A bearing whose running geometry is machined into a stationary bore or face. Sleeve, cylindrical, lemon-bore, multi-lobe and taper-land designs are examples. The surface does not tilt independently like a tilting pad. Read the fixed-profile and tilting-pad comparison.

Lubrication

Flooded Lubrication

An arrangement in which the bearing cavity is substantially filled with lubricant. It is robust and widely used, but high-speed operation can create additional churning and viscous losses.

Operating principle

Fluid-Film Bearing

A bearing in which the stationary and rotating surfaces are separated during normal operation by a load-carrying film of fluid. Oil-film bearings are one subset; other designs can use water, air or process fluid.

Operating principle

Hydrodynamic Lubrication

Relative motion draws lubricant into a converging gap and generates pressure within the film. That internally generated pressure supports the load. Read the hydrodynamic lubrication guide.

Operating principle

Hydrostatic Lubrication

Externally pressurised lubricant supports or lifts the shaft independently of normal running speed. Jacking-oil systems use this principle during start-up, shutdown or low-speed turning.

Machine component

Journal

The cylindrical shaft surface that rotates within a journal bearing. The term describes the shaft surface, not the bearing itself.

Load direction

Journal Bearing

A plain bearing supporting load acting radially across a rotating shaft. Journal bearings include fixed-profile sleeve bearings and tilting-pad journal bearings. See Oiltech's journal bearing range.

Geometry

Leading and Trailing Edge

The leading edge is where the rotating surface first draws lubricant onto a pad or land. The trailing edge is where lubricant leaves the loaded film. They reverse when the direction of rotation reverses.

Lubrication regime

Mixed Lubrication

A regime in which part of the load is carried by the fluid film and part through local surface contact. It can occur during acceleration, coast-down, overload or inadequate film formation.

Lubrication

Oil Film / Oil-Film Bearing

The thin layer of oil separating moving surfaces. An oil-film bearing is a fluid-film bearing specifically using oil; fluid film is the wider term.

Construction

Pad / Shoe

An individual load-carrying segment in a tilting-pad journal or thrust bearing. Pad and shoe are often used interchangeably, although local product terminology may prefer one.

Construction

Pivot

The support about which a bearing pad tilts to form its hydrodynamic wedge. Pivot geometry, alignment freedom and centre or offset position influence pressure, temperature and load distribution.

Bearing family

Plain Bearing

A bearing in which load is supported across sliding surfaces rather than rolling elements. It is the broad family containing bushes, sleeve bearings, journal bearings and many thrust bearings.

Geometry

Preload / Preset

A geometric ratio used particularly in profiled and tilting-pad journal bearings to relate assembled clearance to machined pad clearance. It influences shaft position, stiffness and damping, and is not an externally applied machine load. Read the journal bearing preload guide.

Bearing form

Sleeve Bearing

A fixed-profile journal bearing with a continuous bore around the shaft, often cylindrical or specially profiled. It is a type of journal bearing, but not every journal bearing is a sleeve bearing.

Calculation

Specific Load / Unit Load

Applied load divided by a stated reference area. For journal bearings this is often projected area, diameter times effective length. For thrust bearings it is normally based on active pad area. Always state the area convention.

Rotordynamics

Stiffness and Damping Coefficients

A local linear description of how bearing reaction force changes with small shaft displacement and velocity about an operating point. Direct and cross-coupled terms are used in critical-speed, response and stability calculations. Read the bearing coefficient guide.

Load direction

Thrust Bearing

A bearing supporting load acting along the shaft axis. Designs include plain thrust faces, taper-land bearings and fixed or equalising tilting-pad assemblies. See Oiltech's thrust bearing range.

Bearing form

Tilting-Pad Bearing

A journal or thrust bearing made from independently supported pads that tilt to establish a load-carrying wedge. Pad geometry, pivot type, offset and lubrication are selected for the duty. For radial designs, see the journal bearing type comparison.

Construction

Tri-Metal / Trimetal Bearing

A layered bearing construction with a structural backing, a stronger intermediate bearing-alloy layer and a thin compatible overlay. Tri-metal describes the architecture, not one alloy; exact materials vary by specification.

Material

White Metal / Whitemetal

Common names for the soft metallic lining used on many industrial plain bearings. Whitemetal is common UK industry usage; white metal is the spaced search form. In bearing applications both are broadly synonymous with Babbitt.

Failure terminology

Wipe / Wiping

Directional smearing, displacement or melting of the bearing lining following loss of adequate film separation. It describes the damage appearance; oil starvation, overload, misalignment or overheating may be the initiating cause.

Common Distinctions

Terms That Are Often Confused

Are whitemetal, white metal and Babbitt the same?

Broadly yes in bearing applications. They describe the family of soft bearing alloys used as a lining, but they do not identify the exact alloy grade. Material specification and composition remain important.

Are sleeve and journal bearings the same?

A sleeve bearing is one type of journal bearing. Journal bearing is the broader radial-load category and also includes tilting-pad and other fixed-profile designs.

Are oil-film and fluid-film bearings the same?

Oil-film bearings are fluid-film bearings using oil. Fluid film is broader because water, air or process fluid can also separate the surfaces in suitable designs.

Are hydrodynamic and hydrostatic bearings the same?

No. Hydrodynamic pressure is generated by motion through a converging film. Hydrostatic pressure is supplied externally and can support load without normal running speed.

Terminology Reference

The glossary is aligned with current plain-bearing terminology in ISO 4378-1:2024 for bearing design and materials and ISO 4378-3:2024 for lubrication. Definitions are written for practical industrial use rather than reproduced from the standards.
